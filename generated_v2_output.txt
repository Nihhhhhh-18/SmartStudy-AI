## 1. Summary

- **Overfitting**: Occurs when a machine learning model learns the training data too closely, including noise and unimportant patterns. An overfitted model may perform very well on training data but performs poorly on unseen data.
- **Underfitting**: Occurs when a model fails to learn the important patterns in the training data. An underfitted model typically performs poorly on both training and unseen data.
- **Regularization**: A technique designed to reduce overfitting by discouraging excessive model complexity, which helps the model generalize better to unseen data.
- **Cross-Validation**: A model evaluation method that splits available data into training and validation portions across multiple rounds, helping estimate performance on data not used to fit the model.
- **Other Methods to Reduce Overfitting**: Includes collecting more representative training data, pruning decision trees, and using dropout in suitable neural networks.
- **Generalization**: Refers to a model's ability to perform well on new, unseen examples. A useful machine learning model learns meaningful patterns rather than simply memorizing its training data.
